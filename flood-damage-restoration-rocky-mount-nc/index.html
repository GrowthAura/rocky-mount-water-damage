Rocky Mount's Flood History and Why It Still Matters

Few cities in North Carolina know flooding the way Rocky Mount does. In September 1999, Hurricane Floyd dropped heavy rain on ground already soaked by Hurricane Dennis. The Tar River crested at a record 32.35 feet in Rocky Mount on September 17, and about 2,000 homes in the city were damaged by flooding. Neighborhoods such as Brookview flooded as creeks feeding the Tar River overflowed.

The river has risen again since. Hurricane Matthew pushed it to 28.73 feet in October 2016, and heavy spring rain in April 2017 sent it well over flood stage again, closing roads and pushing water into low-lying areas east of town. Because the Tar River drains a large area upstream, it often crests in Rocky Mount days after the rain stops, which means flood water can keep rising while the sun is out.

That history matters for restoration today. Many homes near the river and its creeks have flooded more than once, older repairs may have hidden damage, and a lot of houses sit on crawl spaces where flood water lingers out of sight. A careful flood cleanup looks at all of it, not just the rooms you can see.

Is Flood Water in Rocky Mount Dangerous?

Short answer: yes. River, creek and street flood water is classified as Category 3, or black water, because it carries sewage, bacteria, fuel, pesticides and debris. Anything porous it soaks, like carpet, drywall and insulation, is usually removed, and every hard surface must be cleaned and disinfected before drying. Keep children and pets out, and wear boots and gloves if you must go in.

  7. 7

Returning Home Safely After a Flood in Rocky Mount

  • Wait until local officials say it is safe to return to your neighborhood.
  • Do not enter if water is still inside or if you see sagging ceilings, shifted walls or foundation damage.
  • Keep the main breaker off until an electrician checks any panel or outlets that were under water.
  • If you smell gas, leave right away and call the gas company from outside.
  • Watch for snakes, fire ants and other animals that moved in with the water.
  • Wear rubber boots, waterproof gloves and an N95 mask when handling anything flood water touched.
  • Photograph every room, the water line on the walls and damaged belongings before you move anything.
  • Run generators only outdoors, far from doors and windows.
  • Throw out food, medicine and cosmetics that flood water touched.

What Can Be Saved After Flood Water Gets Into a Rocky Mount Home

  • Concrete, brick, tile, metal and solid wood framing: cleaned, disinfected and dried in place.
  • Drywall and wall insulation: removed to a clean line above the water mark.
  • Carpet and pad: removed and discarded.
  • Hardwood and laminate flooring: usually removed if submerged; subfloor dried and checked.
  • Cabinets: solid wood boxes may be saved; particleboard swells and is replaced.
  • Crawl space insulation and vapor barrier: removed and replaced after drying.
  • Upholstered furniture and mattresses: usually not salvageable after contaminated water.
  • Photos, documents and keepsakes: freeze or air-dry quickly; some can be professionally restored.
  • HVAC ducts that were submerged: flex duct replaced, metal duct cleaned or replaced, system inspected before running.

We photograph and list everything we remove so nothing is missed on your claim.

Flash Flooding and Street Flooding in Rocky Mount

You do not have to live beside the river to flood. Summer thunderstorms can drop several inches of rain in an hour or two, and Rocky Mount's creeks and storm drains fill fast. During past events, roads and parking lots around Sunset Avenue and Buck Leonard Boulevard disappeared under water, and roads east of the city such as Leggett Road, Cox Avenue and Gay Road have been closed by flooding.

Flash flood water often enters through doorways, garages, crawl space vents and low windows. It may only be a few inches deep and gone in a few hours, but it leaves behind mud and contamination in the crawl space and on floors. These smaller floods deserve the same careful cleanup as a major river flood, because the mold and odor problems that follow are just as real.

Flood Insurance and FEMA Help in Rocky Mount, Nash and Edgecombe Counties

A standard homeowners policy in North Carolina generally does not cover flooding from rivers, creeks or surface water. That coverage usually comes from a separate flood policy, most often through the National Flood Insurance Program, and new NFIP policies typically have a waiting period before they take effect, so it pays to buy before storm season. When a major disaster is declared for Nash or Edgecombe County, FEMA assistance may also be available to help with essential repairs for eligible households.

Whichever path applies to you, good documentation speeds things up. We photograph the water line in every room, record moisture readings, list every material removed and keep samples when an adjuster asks. If your home sits near the Tar River or Stoney Creek and you do not yet have flood coverage, it is worth a conversation with your agent before the next hurricane season.

Flood Damage FAQs for Rocky Mount, NC

Answers to what Twin Counties homeowners ask most after high water.

How soon does mold grow after a flood in Rocky Mount?

In Rocky Mount's warm, humid climate, mold can begin growing on wet drywall, wood and insulation within 24 to 48 hours. After a flood, homes often sit closed up for days before owners can return, so visible growth is common. Fast removal of wet materials and aggressive drying are the best defense.

Can drywall be dried and saved after flooding?

Drywall that soaked up outside flood water is usually cut out, because it is porous and absorbs contaminants along with the water. We cut at a clean line above the highest wicking point, often a couple of feet above the water line, so the framing behind can be cleaned, disinfected and dried.

Do I need to remove all my flooring after a flood?

Not always. Carpet and pad are removed after flood water. Tile on concrete can usually be cleaned. Hardwood and laminate often have to go if they sat in contaminated water, and the subfloor beneath them must be checked and dried. We decide room by room based on readings, not guesses.

How soon can you start after flood water goes down?

As soon as officials say it is safe to return and the water has drained from your home. In a large event across the Twin Counties, demand spikes, so calling early gets you on the schedule. We can often start documentation and mud-out the same day we arrive.

Is flood water in my crawl space as serious as water inside the house?

Yes. Flood water under the house is just as contaminated as flood water inside it, and it sits right against your joists, subfloor and ductwork. The insulation and vapor barrier usually need to come out, the soil surface needs cleaning and the space needs thorough drying before new materials go in.
